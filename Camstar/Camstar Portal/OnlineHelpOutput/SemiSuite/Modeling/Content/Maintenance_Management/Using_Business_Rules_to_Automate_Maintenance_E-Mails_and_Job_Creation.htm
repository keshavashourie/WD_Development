Business rules enable you to add custom processing logic to the Opcenter Execution Semiconductor application. This table shows each business rule modeling object and the sample instance provided to assist with maintenance.

You define custom logic in a Business Rule Handler and then associate the business rule handler to a Business Rule for maintenance purposes. You then schedule the business rule for automated execution using the Scheduled Business Rule.

Note: The delivery of e-mail is controlled by the Notification Server and the configured SMTP server within Modeling. Refer to the Opcenter Execution Core Installation Guide for information.
Before Defining Business Rule Handlers

Customers can have hundreds and thousands of equipment items assigned to groups that align with manufacturing processes. Siemens recommends that you create parent equipment groups that encompass the smaller groups so that you won't have to create a business rule set for individual equipment groups.

This diagram illustrates the process of defining a parent group and configuring the objects required for automated e-mail notifications and maintenance job creation. The process gets repeated for each parent group.

How to Define the Automated Notification Business Rule Handler

Note: The instructions below are based on the assumption that you have assigned date or recurring date maintenance requirements (or both) to equipment groups or maintenance classes.

Follow these steps to define the automated notification business rule handler:

1. Open the Business Rule Handler page. The Business Rule Handler page appears within the Modeling page.
2. Select the provided instance: StateChangeMaintainHandler.
3. Click Copy. The Copy pop-up appears.
4. Enter a new Target Instance Name related to the specific equipment group, for example, PARENT_EQUIPMENT_GROUP01_BRH.
5. Click Copy. The pop-up closes.
6. In Script area: locate the line: GetNamedObject("SMTPTransportName","SMTPTransport",Transaction::EmailTransport);
Replace "SMTPTransportName" with the name of the SMTP Transport configured in Modeling.
7. In Script area locate the line: InitQueryParametersEx("ResourceGroupName","%",CLF::Param). Replace the % symbol with your actual equipment group name, for example, "PARENT_EQUIPMENT_GROUP_01" within quotation marks.

Using this example, the line would look as follows:
InitQueryParametersEx("ResourceGroupName","PARENT_EQUIPMENT_GROUP_01",CLF::Param);

8. Click Save. The application saves the modeling object and displays a success message.

How to Define the Automated Notification Business Rule

Follow these steps to define the Automated Notification Business Rule:

1. Open the Business Rule page. The Business Rule page appears within the Modeling page.
2. Select the provided instance: StateChangeMaintainRules.
3. Click Copy. The Copy pop-up appears.
4. Enter a new Target Instance Name related to the specific equipment group, for example, PARENT_EQUIPMENT_GROUP01_BR.
5. Click Copy. The pop-up closes.
6. Delete all content from the Handler grid. For each row with content:
a. Select the row.
b. Click Delete selected row on the Handler grid toolbar. The application deletes the selected row from the grid.
7. Click Add new row on the Handler grid toolbar. A new row appears.
8. Enter the name of the Business Rule Handler in the Handler column.
9. Click Save. The application saves the modeling object and displays a success message.

How to Define the Automated Notification Scheduled Business Rule

Follow these steps to define the Automated Notification Scheduled Business Rule:

1. Open the Scheduled Business Rule page. The Scheduled Business Rule page appears within the Modeling page.
2. Select the provided instance: StateChangeMaintain.
3. Click Copy. The Copy pop-up appears.
4. Enter a new Target Instance Name related to the specific equipment group, for example, PARENT_EQUIPMENT_GROUP01_SBR.
5. Click Copy. The pop-up closes.
6. Select the appropriate business rule from the Business Rule field. (Following the example, this would be the rule PARENT_EQUIPMENT_GROUP01_BR that was created earlier.)
7. Enter scheduling information according to the Pending, Due, and Past Due settings of your installation's maintenance requirements.
8. Click Save. The application saves the modeling object and displays a success message.
Note: When creating scheduled business rules, keep in mind to space the scheduling to minimize impact on server performance.
