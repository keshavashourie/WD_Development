Understanding Parts Management

The term "part" refers to an interchangeable physical object that is commonly used on resources or equipments. Parts can be stored in an inventory, moved from inventory to production and back. It can also be known as spare part, service part, repair part or replacement part.

A typical factory may store numbers of resources (equipments, machines, etc.) and each resource may comprise numerous quantities and varieties of parts. Failure to supply and ensure readiness of the parts may adversely affect the operating process. Therefore, it is crucial to ensure respective parts function as intended without failure for the desired period during their design life.

Parts Management provides a controlled sequence of transactions to track the parts from inventory to production and vice versa. For example, a request to release a part to production, a request to return the part back to inventory. The services help to support tracking of the part’s expiry date and limits such as usage, lifetime, and recondition. Email notification will be triggered when there is a limit violation.

The following outlines the benefits of implementing Parts Management:

• Ensure parts availability– Parts Management supports tracking of inventory levels and auto generation of warning through email. Email notifications can be auto forwarded to designated recipients to highlight the need to replenish when the level reaches certain defined level. This will prevent stock-out issues and ensure stocks availability.
• Ensure parts reliability– Parts Management supports recondition limit feature where you can set usage limitation of parts. For example, Part A is set to have recondition limit of 200 use. When the part hits the recondition limit, the part is automatically decommissioned.
• Allow part tracking– Controlled sequence of transactions must be executed when a requested part is taken out from inventory or when it is returned to the inventory as well as when it is decommissioned.
• Avoid extended downtime– Time spent in locating the part is actually added to the downtime of the equipment. The Parts Management feature supports the tracking of the inventory location of the part and therefore, time searching for parts is minimized.
• Increase accuracy– The Parts Management feature provides immediate access to a Bill of Materials (resource parts), so technicians can be sure they get the right part.

Before executing part transactions, parts modeling objects must be configured. Resource Material Part is a required modeling object during part creation where it will inherit characteristics of the associated Resource Material Parts such as function, usage limit and expiry date. Lists of modeled resource material parts can be viewed in Material Parts page.

Parts Management Transaction Pages

Parts Management transaction pages provide the capabilities to define and control parts assigned to a resource. These pages are accessed from the Parts Services menu—Core > Resource > Parts > Parts Services, if you are using the default menu structure. The following explains the summary of part's related pages and their functions:

• Material Parts page— To search and view existing resource material parts after they are modeled with the Material Part modeling object. You can configure Part Create from this page.
• Part Maintenance page— To search and view existing parts. You can configure Part Create, Part Setup and Part Scrap from this page.
• Part Create page— To create a new part. After the part is created, modifications can be made in Part modeling page.
• Part Scrappage— To scrap quantity from part. The part will be decommissioned if the entire quantity is scrapped.
• Part Request page— To request resource material parts from inventory or to return resource material parts from a resource back to inventory.
• Part Request Acknowledge page— To acknowledge a part request.
• Part Request Assign page— To assign resource material parts to a part request.
• Part Request Issue page— To issue requested parts to a resource after the parts have been assigned.
• Part Request Cancel page— To cancel a part request.
• Part Request Cancel Acknowledge page— To acknowledge the cancellation of a parts request.
• Part Request Update page— To update a part request.
• Part Request Complete page— To verify that all requested parts in the requested quantities have been met for a part request and to update the request status to Complete.
• Part Setup page— To assign a setup to the part. Setup helps to link the part to a physical machine configurations and processes.

Defining Resource Material Parts before Using Part Management Transactions

Each part defined in the application must be associated with a resource material part. This association facilitates parts management, as multiple parts may share the same maintenance and other requirements such as limits. Resource material parts are defined in modeling and include the following:

• Usage Limit—The amount of thruput that a part may process after which the part must be reconditioned before further use.
• Recondition Limit—The maximum number of times a part may be reconditioned after which it must be retired.
• Lifetime Limit—Limit past which a part must be decommissioned and no longer used.
• Expiry Date—Limit past which a part must be decommissioned and no longer used.
• Part Type—Serialized, Non-Serialized, Bulk.
Note: When usage limit is met, the part can no longer be used and must be reconditioned. And when the part hits the recondition limit, it must be entirely retired. A part cannot be used when it reaches its lifetime limit or expiry date.
