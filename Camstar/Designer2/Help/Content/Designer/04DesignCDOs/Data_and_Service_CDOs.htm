Data and Service CDOs

The Opcenter EX MDD and Opcenter EX CR object model is based on standard object-oriented concepts and practices. CDO definitions are synonymous with classes. Objects derived from the BaseObject are its subclasses. Each BaseObject subclass can also have its own subclass. The typical way to customize the Opcenter EX MDD or Opcenter EX CR structure is to define CDOs and add fields. There are two categories of CDOs: Data and Service.

Data CDOs

Data CDOs define the entities in your system and their behavior in the form of CLFs. Data CDOs include:

• Product
• Workflow
• Equipment
• Material

Data CDOs are persistent objects in the system; its data is non-volatile and continues beyond system restarts. The entirety of data CDOs define the state of the system. Service CDOs and CLFs are used to alter the state of an object.

Service CDOs

Service CDOs define the tasks or unit of work you can perform on data CDOs. Service CDOs include:

• Product Maintenance (to add product definition)
• Move (to move a part to a downstream workflow step)
• Collect Data (to capture quality and parametric data)

CLFs define the behavior of service CDOs. Service CDOs are not persistent, but alter the persistent state of the system by operating on data CDOs.

The unit of work performed by a service CDO is executed as a transaction under the ACID principle (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, and Durability). This means that either all alterations to the system state get committed as a whole or none get committed.

Fields

Data and service CDOs are predefined in Opcenter EX MDD and Opcenter EX CR, and support these field types:

• Boolean (True or False)
• Decimal (fixed point number type with precision and scale)
• Fixed (fixed point number type)
• Float (approximate number type)
• Integer (whole number type)
• String (text)
• Timestamp (date and time)
• Object (CDO)

Opcenter EX MDD and Opcenter EX CR also support scalar fields and list fields. Following the model-driven architecture, a field can have CLFs attached to define custom behavior in response to events in the system, like retrieving the field's value.
